# Story sources

Science Messenger covers science news, and only from sources with a strong record for accuracy. Every story must trace back to primary material: the peer-reviewed paper, the agency data release or the official mission statement. Press releases and news coverage help you find stories, but they are not where the facts come from.

## Picking topics
Take stories that are both **solid** and **interesting**. If one of the two is missing, skip it.
- **Solid:** published in a peer-reviewed journal, released by a space or science agency, or confirmed by at least two of the outlets below. No preprint-only stories unless they're clearly labeled as not yet peer reviewed.
- **Interesting:** there's a clear "what did they find" hook and a reason the reader should care.
- **Skip:** single-study miracle cures, "X causes cancer" from small or animal-only studies, company press releases with no data, and anything that only appears on tabloids or aggregators.
- **Picture:** a free-licensed Commons image that shows the subject (the telescope, the animal, the organ, the place).

## Writing rules: accurate, own words, no copyrighted material
- Rewrite every story completely in our own words. Never copy or lightly reword a source's sentences.
- Combine facts from at least 2 sources, ideally the paper itself and one credible news report, and list them all under sources. Link the paper by DOI or journal page when there is one.
- State the limits: sample size, animal vs human, observational vs causal, preprint vs peer reviewed. Name the journal and the lead institution.
- No hype words ("breakthrough", "cure", "miracle", "proves") unless a named expert in the sources says so, and then quote them.
- Quotes: only short ones the sources actually contain, attributed, and at most one or two per story. Paraphrase everything else.
- Images: only free-licensed Wikimedia Commons files with the author and license credited. NASA/ESA/NOAA public-domain or CC images on Commons are ideal. Never use photos from news sites, journals, social media or agencies outside Commons.
- Headlines and decks are ours. They can pose a question or tease the finding, but they must be literally true and must not overstate the result. Don't reuse the source's headline.

## Primary sources (most credible)
- Journals: [Nature](https://www.nature.com/news), [Science](https://www.science.org/news), [Cell](https://www.cell.com/), [The Lancet](https://www.thelancet.com/), [NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/), [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/), [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/ncomms/), [Current Biology](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/), [The BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/), [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/), [Physical Review Letters](https://journals.aps.org/prl/), [The Astrophysical Journal](https://iopscience.iop.org/journal/0004-637X)
- Agencies: [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news/), [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Newsroom), [JAXA](https://global.jaxa.jp/press/), [NOAA](https://www.noaa.gov/news), [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/news), [WHO](https://www.who.int/news), [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/media/), [NIH](https://www.nih.gov/news-events), [CERN](https://home.cern/news), [ESO](https://www.eso.org/public/news/), [Copernicus Climate Change Service](https://climate.copernicus.eu/), [WMO](https://wmo.int/media/news)
- Research news: [EurekAlert! (AAAS)](https://www.eurekalert.org/) for embargoed journal releases, [university newsrooms](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/browse) for the lead institution's own account

## Science journalism (to find stories and cross-check)
- Wire services: [Reuters Science](https://www.reuters.com/science/), [AP Science](https://apnews.com/science)
- Public broadcasters: [BBC Science](https://www.bbc.com/news/science_and_environment), [NPR Science](https://www.npr.org/sections/science/), [PBS NewsHour Science](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/science)
- Newspapers: [The Guardian Science](https://www.theguardian.com/science), [The New York Times Science](https://www.nytimes.com/section/science)
- Specialist outlets: [Quanta Magazine](https://www.quantamagazine.org/), [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/), [New Scientist](https://www.newscientist.com/), [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/), [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/) (health), [Ars Technica Science](https://arstechnica.com/science/), [Space.com](https://www.space.com/), [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/) (climate), [Knowable Magazine](https://knowablemagazine.org/)
- Fact-checking: [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/), [Health News Review archive](https://www.healthnewsreview.org/), [Science Feedback](https://science.feedback.org/)

## Verification checklist
1. Find the paper or official release and read at least the abstract and methods summary.
2. Confirm the publication date and that it's peer reviewed (or say clearly that it isn't).
3. Check that the numbers in the story match the paper, not just the press release.
4. Look for an independent expert comment in a credible outlet and include their view when there is one.
5. Check Retraction Watch for the authors or the paper if the claim is surprising.
