After 26 years, the US is officially no longer measles-free

A regional review found that one chain of measles infections kept spreading in the country for more than a year without a break. Mexico and Bolivia lost the same status.

By Farlight Staff · November 19, 2026 · Pan American Health Organization, United States · 3 min read

The United States has lost the measles elimination status it first earned in 2000. The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), the World Health Organization's regional office for the Americas, announced the change on 17 November 2026. Mexico and Bolivia lost their status at the same time.

The decision followed a meeting from 10 to 13 November of an independent panel, the Regional Monitoring and Re-Verification Commission. It reviews evidence from 47 countries and territories in the Americas and advises PAHO's director, Jarbas Barbosa, who accepted its recommendations.

What 'elimination' actually means

Elimination does not mean zero cases. Travellers can still bring the virus in, and outbreaks can still happen. A country only loses the label when a single chain of infections, caused by the same genetic lineage of the virus, keeps spreading without interruption for 12 months or longer.

According to PAHO, the US chain began on 20 January 2025 and crossed the 12-month line on 20 January 2026. Mexico's began on 1 February 2025. Canada lost its own status in November 2025, after an outbreak that started in October 2024, and that loss also cost the Americas as a whole their regional elimination status.

The numbers behind the decision are stark. The CDC counted 5,412 confirmed US cases in 2026 as of 12 November, reported from 47 jurisdictions, with 43 new outbreaks this year. That compares with 2,289 cases in all of 2025. The CDC lists six deaths so far in 2026, while state health officials have reported about 10, a gap IFLScience attributes to the CDC's narrower definition. Across the Americas, PAHO counted 58,213 cases this year.

"Elimination is not a trophy that we win once and put on a shelf." (Jarbas Barbosa, PAHO director)

Can the status be won back?

Yes, but it takes time. A country has to stop the endemic chain for at least 12 consecutive months and show that its vaccination, disease surveillance and outbreak response have been strengthened, including a corrective action plan reviewed by the commission. Venezuela and Brazil lost their status in 2018 and 2019 and both had it restored in 2024. Canada declared its outbreak over on 19 October 2026, but it still has to go a full year without spread of that strain.

Vaccination is the main lever. Health officials consider about 95% coverage with two doses of the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine necessary to stop spread in every community. Among US kindergarteners in the 2025 to 2026 school year, coverage was 92.4%, according to figures cited by Scientific American, and some states sit far lower.

The label itself changes nothing on the ground: the virus spreads wherever people are unprotected, whatever the official status. Anyone unsure whether they are protected can check their records with a doctor or local health department.
